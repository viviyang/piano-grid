**Title:** Piano Finger Numbers: Left & Right Hand Chart

**Description:** Learn piano finger numbers 1 to 5 for both hands. Read RH and LH examples and tell fingering apart from scale degrees and octave labels.

**Scope:** Finger-number identification; not a universal fingering chart for every chord, scale or song.

## Both thumbs are 1

On each hand, 1 means thumb, 2 index finger, 3 middle finger, 4 ring finger and 5 little finger. The numbering does not continue from 5 on the left hand to 6 on the right. Each hand has its own 1–5 set.

LH means left hand; RH means right hand. Read the hand label before interpreting a number. LH 1 means left thumb, while RH 1 means right thumb.


## Read the two hands from the player’s view

The planned diagram uses the view of a player looking down at the backs of both hands. With the left hand drawn on the left and the right hand on the right, the two thumbs sit toward the middle.

Across the left-hand diagram, the displayed finger labels run 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 from left to right. Across the right-hand diagram, they run 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. These are identification labels, not a prescribed posture or an anatomical teaching model.


## A finger number is not a permanent key assignment

Finger 1 does not always play C. A fingering instruction chooses a digit for a note in a particular passage. A different register, neighboring notes or hand may call for a different choice.

A chord’s root and its lowest note are also not finger numbers. In an inversion, the chord member in the bass changes, while the numbering of your hands remains the same.


## Keep three kinds of numbers apart

Finger 3 identifies the middle finger of the specified hand. Scale degree 3 identifies the third degree of a scale. The 4 in C4 identifies an octave register. None of these numbers can be substituted for another.

For example, E is the third degree of C major. That does not require every E to be played with finger 3. C4 remains the same pitch label whichever digit presses the key.


## Try four reading checks

Cover the answers, interpret each instruction, then compare. These questions check labels rather than piano technique.

1. LH 1 → left thumb.
2. RH 5 → right little finger.
3. RH 3 → right middle finger.
4. C4 → the note C in octave 4, not finger 4.

## Look up fingering for an exact example

After learning the labels, choose a page that states the chord or scale, hand, direction where relevant, and octave range. Read its source and limitation. A generic hand chart alone cannot supply all of those decisions.

Use the existing C-major chord page for a root-position example and the C-major scale page for its own stated scale pattern. Do not assume the chord’s 1–3–5 pattern is the scale’s complete fingering.


## Questions and answers



**Is the left thumb number 5?**

No. Both thumbs are 1. The left hand looks reversed on the page, but its numbering by finger identity does not change.

**Does LH 3 mean the third key from the left?**

No. It means the middle finger of the left hand. A separate note name or notation tells you which pitch to play.

**Does this chart tell me how to finger any song?**

No. It explains the numbers. A complete passage may require its own context-specific fingering.
