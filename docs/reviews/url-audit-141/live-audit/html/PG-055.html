C-flat major contains C-flat, E-flat and G-flat. On an equal-tempered piano, its pitch classes match B, D-sharp and F-sharp. The displayed root-position example is C♭4–E♭4–G♭4. Numbers after note letters identify octaves, not fingers. Use the diagram, note list and bass label together; Play chord sounds the selected notes together, while the note-by-note control sounds them from low to high.

What to notice

A flat sign does not guarantee a black key. C-flat uses the white B key immediately below the written C. In this root example, C♭4 is the same sounding key as B3, not B4. C-flat to E-flat is a major third and C-flat to G-flat a perfect fifth. The three flat signs preserve the root–third–fifth letter pattern C–E–G. Replacing only one label with its sharp equivalent would obscure that spelling.

Find the notes on your keyboard

  1. Locate middle C, labeled C4. Move one semitone lower to the B3 key; read it here as C♭4.
  2. Find E♭4 and G♭4 on black keys.
  3. Read C♭4–E♭4–G♭4 before playing. The written octave number follows the letter C even when the accidental crosses the C/B boundary.

Match the octave before comparing with B major

C♭4–E♭4–G♭4 and B3–D♯4–F♯4 have identical MIDI values: 59, 63 and 66. They describe the same physical keys with different spellings.

The prepared B-major detail page starts its root example one octave higher. Compare matched registers manually rather than assuming two page defaults should produce identical pitches.

A reference for spelling, not a new tuning system

The examples use the site’s ordinary equal-tempered pitch mapping. Keeping C-flat labels does not require a special instrument or a different keyboard layout.

The first inversion is E♭4–G♭4–C♭5, labeled C♭/E♭. C♭5 uses the B4 key. Inversion and octave labels must both remain correct when these notes reach the renderer.

Questions about C flat major

Is C-flat a black key?

No. C-flat is the white key immediately below C, normally labeled B in the same sounding register.

Why is C-flat4 MIDI 59?

Octave numbers are assigned by the written letter. Lowering C4, MIDI 60, by one semitone gives C-flat4, MIDI 59, the B3 key.

Is C-flat major different from C major?

Yes. C major is C–E–G; C-flat major is C-flat–E-flat–G-flat. Every corresponding pitch is one semitone lower.

Are hand-specific fingerings available?

No verified hand-number examples have been prepared for this page. The missing evidence is not filled by copying another chord’s hand numbers.

Does the keyboard builder recognize enharmonic choices?

The existing builder checks sounding pitch classes rather than written spelling. C-flat and B can therefore count as the same pitch class; this is not a notation-spelling examination.
